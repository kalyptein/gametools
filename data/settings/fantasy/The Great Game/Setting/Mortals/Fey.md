### The Three Courts

* sun, moon, and stars
	* for elves, this affects their glamour and appearance (the moonshadows of moon elves, etc)
* 
* Sun: grandeur, revelry, pomp, etiquette and courtliness, tradition, history, holding to the past
	* least comfortable underground
* 
* Moon: secrets, mystery, subtlety, and stealth, inscrutable and aloof, schemes and plans, looking to the future
	* also mercurial?  reflecting the changing moon?
* 
* Stars: walk in the light and dark, roam the wilds, passionate and primal, living in the present

* fey may feel called into one of the courts for certain traits, while not vibing with all of them, perhaps even clashing.
	* fey can switch courts if they feel the call (not to say the court-mates they've "abandoned" might not hold some resentment); it's uncommon but hardly unknown.

### The Wild Hunt

- originated in the Age of Twilight in a much more "social club" form, chivalric hunters who pursued worth quarry, boasted and toasted one another, roamed and explored.
	- Court of Stars was the plurality, but it spanned all three courts.
- It transformed quickly and savagely into an army to fight a slowly losing war against the demons as the Age of Night took hold.
- it still exists across the modern Fulcrum World, with various regional variations and attitudes.  Serves as a force of rangers, protecting fey lands, bearing messages between faeholds, and spying out threats.  They still maintain their hunting traditions, but with a grimmer character than originally, particularly pursuing demons.

### Na Tillteanaich

* (nuh CHEEL-chuh-nich)
* The Returners (Poetic, melancholy; implies returning to our rightful world)
	* often translated The Reclaimers; might be a shorted form of The Who Return to the True World.

- A cabal of fey, sometimes secret, sometimes not, who want to regather fragments of the wode and remake the world as it was in the Age of Twilight.
	- This will probably cause some kind of terrible cosmic disaster if they ever succced.
- Often villainous, though other fey may find them sympathetic, even if they reject them.
- In a lot of cases, the Twilight fragments are down in the Underworld, where they have become corrupted, and dwelling there will tend to lead to corruption of the would-be reclaimers (and thus a corrupted Twilight if they ever succeed).
- 
- more of an ideology that a singular group, though aligned groups often cooperate

### Hags & Witches

* During the age of night, some fey entered or were forced into service to some of the self-restraint-capable demon lords.
	* One Grazz't-like / Black Man lord (very cunning and a scholar of occult lore and power) collected women (all kith) to his service and taught them demonology, which they added to their powers of glamour.  They served him for protection and power.
* The fey women were transformed gradually by demonic association into hags, growing monstrous, eating flesh for both power and pleasure.
* The demon lord was one of the players in the age-ending struggle for ascension.
	* At the final hour, the chief of his witches,  the one who had begun his coven and first gathered others, broke away and stole enough power for her own ascension, aided by fellow witch conspirators.
	* The demon lord did retain enough power to ascend, but was lessened from what he might have been.
* The witch-goddess and the black man hate and oppose each other to this day.

* any kith of fey can become a hag
	* physically monstrous (though as fey they can often transform or disguise themselves), with talons, sharp teeth, etc.
	* eat flesh (and perhaps other parts of beings), their demonic taint allowing them to digest power and eldritch nourishment from their victims
* the greatest hags are those who survived from the age of night (and thus from the age of twilight)
	* some serve the witch-goddess or black man, others are largely independent

## The Common Kith


* Fey are not bound to discrete types, they are fluid and malleable, both in person and across generations.
* The "common" kith all arose early in the Age of Twilight, so they had a lot of time to produce decedents in their rough mold.
* There was presumably some kind of ur-fey.  Exactly what they were like is uncertain.  They are sometimes depicted in a kind of amalgamation of common kith traits: unusually tall elves, horned, with a scattering of animal features, and sometimes wings.

### Elves


- elves are adept at molding matter and energy, including biology.

- high (sun), wold (star), shadow (moon) elves
- The Wode: before the Sun and Moon, they ruled an elder order of the world, the Wode.
- ???Now the Wode drifts beyond the edges, impinging here and there. Some elves accept the change, others scheme to regain mastery.

- When the Wode went adrift, the elves split into 3 to find sanctuary
    - high elves (sun elves) leaned toward law, dwelling in fragments of Wode hung in the firmament, celestial palaces; they are formal, with high etiquette and elaborate arts; sustaining and endlessly reflecting and recapitulating the wode. Limned by the golden light of the ever-setting Sun of the Age of Twilight.

	- wild elves (star elves) leaned toward chaos, dwelling in fragments of Wode lose in dream and the unknown, memories of wild places; they are merry and feral, hunting, reveling, traveling; memorializing the fading wode with revels, gathering in its pieces, defending it from outsiders. Lit by the stars which glitter reflected in their eyes, dappled in the Wode's shadow, from the Age of Twilight.

    - shadow elves (moon elves?) leaned toward entropy, dwelling in fragments of the Wode drawn into the Underworld, decaying glories of the past; they are morbid and cruel, amused by death and failure, intriguing over claims to the shrinking wode. They are lit only by the silver-monochrome light of the horizon-bound Moon of the Age of Twilight, with the shadows of night cast across the rest of them.
        - The cast of shadows upon them is generally fixed, and is assigned an astrological-like meaning about their lives, personality, role to play, etc (face in shadow, fully-lit, etc). It can change in dramatic moments, or sometimes as they grow up.

- They reproduce by the branching time-streams of the Wode, new instances with variation (different choices, different gender, etc) like multiversal versions. Sometimes this is done on purpose, often by acceident. They cannot reproduce without access Wode, and so have declined with time.
    - suggests elves are ageless, not just long-lived.

### Gnomes

### Sprites

- tiny winged luminous folk
	- sprite wings vary across the range of possibilities; feathered, bat-like, insect-like, luminous diablo-angle-tentacle-wings, shimmering clouds of motes, etc.

- adept at manipulating thought and emotion

- they bud from dreamtime.
	- start out dreamy and flighty and fairly single/simple emotion/through focused.
	- as they grow in power, they also grow in mental complexity and physical size (still pretty small, but going from about 1' to up toward 2')

### Sluagh

- demon-corrupted fey, rare that the fragile gossamer of fey survives admixture with demonic corruption
- maddened and ever-hungry for more Twilight, eager to devour fey; the demonic infection slowly burns them away
- gestalt things, hosts of devoured fey-minds, erratic but cunning, with all the tricks and knowledge they've devoured, shifting between cloud, flock, swarm, and shadow
- will gnaw on mortals too
- very dangerous, but if defeated, a lot of Twilight can be harvested out of them; the Wild Hunt and the Reclaimers sometime dare it; for the reward, and to put the fragments of its fey to rest
- when Sluagh get big enough, sometimes they tear into multiple Sluagh, particularly if they're composed of multiple powerful fey-mind-spirits
- Some sluagh can converse coherent, plot and plan, never trustworthy, the most dangerous of their kind, usually inhabited by one or more great fey lords, from whom they take a corrupted persona
- they wield degrees of both glamour and demonic magic
- they don't tend to hang around with other demons, feeling no kinship toward them; they are only rivals for the feast
