
- The turkish-inflected names for cosmic stuff are the result of the elder races being middle eastern in flavor (at least linguistically); the first empires, and the so-far-greatest in magical power/lore.
- Some of these beings have diverged since the dawn of time.
    - fallen malakim, law-abiding djinn, benevolent ghul


## Djinn (chaos)

- passionate and creative
- multiple arms (not always 4), sometimes tentacles, claws, crab claws, etc), kind of like hindu gods.
- fantastical features: elemental, beastial, mechanical, etc
- relegated to the underworld and the isles of the edges of the world, but still very powerful. their ruins and still-ruled domains exist in far places
- djinn imparted the ways of elemental magic
- most surviving djinn have retreated out toward the horizon, dwelling in the isles nearer to chaos
- abandoned djinn palaces and the like remain in the world, usually in the heart of some turbulent elemental phenomenon (volcano, seething jungle, arctic waste, etc)
- There are djinn who have found pleasure in order, becoming scholars, rulers, etc.
- multi-armed; more arms = greater power / status?
- chimerical animal and elemental features (fire hair, horns, stone-skin, leafy hair, snake tongue, hooves, fish-head, goat-head, fur, tails, etc)
- 
- djinn - collective
- djinni - singular
- djinniyya - feminine singular


## Malākim (law)

- (Sarufiyyun, Musharifin? Karubiyin)
- in islam: malak (either from malaka = "he controlled", or from a root meaning messenger)
- expressions of duty, law, meaning
- singular: Malāk
- many winged, many eyed, cosmic/celestial vibes, humanoid or weird, many-eyed (crystal eyes?), crystals, sigiled skin, floating emtal and crystal halos, luminous, celestial motifs (sun, moon, star light, patterns, etc)
- malakim imparted the meditative ways of psionics
    - chaos created "corrupt" forms based on passion and frenzy; ardents
    - entropy created "corrupt" forms based on madness and noovore-ing
- some malakim survive in the upper skies, but many of their crystal palaces, citadels, and tombs drift abandoned in the lower sky, or have even fallen to earth.
- There are fallen seraphs, given in to passions and disorder, pursuing lovers and breeding nephelim (origin of giantkind? maybe a hybrid of seraph and djinn?).

- given the avian motif of the heavens (phoenix totem beasts), malakim have some bird-features: feathers, sometimes bird heads, maybe talons, bird eyes? although still very biblical angel-looking

## Ghūl (entropy)

- the fallen djinn and seraphim from the war were stitched together into the morbid, necro-cenobite ghul, inhabited by formless hungry spirits of the abyss. they still make lesser ghul servitors from mortal and beast amalgams
- ghul taught magics of devouring, decaying, and repurposing; necromancy, demonology, etc
    - wardogs are creations of ghul-derived necrosurgery? or demonic fusion?
- daelkyr-like makers of horrors and nightmares
- Law and Chaos has bent undeath to their ends, creating revenants
- the surviving ghul tend to lurk in the depths, slumbering and plotting in dream between rising to devour
- ghul redouts from the war remain in the upper underworld, and occasionally in desolate places of the surface world
- are there ghul who serve peaceful ends, making art, preserving knowledge or legacy?
- 
- Ghīlān (ghee-LAHN) - plural for ghul
- Ghūla - feminine singular
