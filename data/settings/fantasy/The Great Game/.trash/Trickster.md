## The five trickeries (the five fingers?  The Trickster's Hand?)

- a trickster buys in to the various Fingers and their sub-abilities.  They may also gain specialities in particular situation, which can buff powers from multiple Fingers.
- feats start out close to mundane, and grow more exotic and fantastical

the backlash for overexerting you power is that the world gets a bit fed up with your bullshit and you get comeuppance.  the backlash comes at the attempt to use a power, and is flavored by that power

too many lies
- the world lies to you (an illusion) and you buy it
- you lose the ability to tell lies or tell when someone is lying
- a lie of your falls apart dramatically, in front of everyone
- you believe a delusion

keen eye
- your sight lies to you
- you get paranoid
- everyone has keen eye on you, you can't lie to or hide from them

light foot
- the world is keeping an eye on you, you become heavy, sticky and obvious

sticky fingers
- some of your possessions go missing
- you lose some intangible, your own face, memory, etc

strange wagers
- losing is its own punishment, or maybe you are fined a bunch of fool's gold

### Strange Wagers / Fortune's Favor

* can gamble, dance with lady luck (the Wheel of Fortune) or other arcana (chess w/ Death?), wager and win unusual things (youth, secrets, influence over someone), or come to grief.  these can even be spur-of-the-moment gambles between you and the world 
* also more generally "strange luck", sometimes "dumb luck" escapes


- Fool's Gold: you can gamble with fate and store up a currency, Fool's Gold, that you spend to fuel greater feats of the various fingers or buy later good fortune.  You gamble by putting yourself in peril on a dare (vowing to acquire something, outsmart a threat, deliver an insult, sneaking into a place/past a threat).  The doing or the having done must always put you in peril; the greater the peril, the greater the reward.  Success, even if the trickster remains endangered (fleeing, having gained an enemy, etc), immediately grants the reward.

- you can spend Fool's Gold to buy turns of fortune (the lucky presence of some aid or escape), or perform greater feats of the other Fingers.  The Shadow is not swayed by Fool's Gold.

- indebted: when you gamble with someone for ordinary things, you can gain a subtle hold on them (you can cheat, but you can't use magic or Fool's Gold to do it).  by forgiving their debt, you can get them to spill a secret or grant a favor.  If its something extreme that they'd never divulge / hand over, they'll do it but immediately realize they shouldn't and perhaps even blame magic, they may attack and try to take it back.  If it's less extreme, they'll do it and think it's their own idea.
	- if you hold a debt from someone, maybe your powers work better on them?  but you must accept repayment if they offer it in full.

- in occult moments (perhaps when the Wheel or one of its Immanents is manifest? or you're in one of its places of power?) you can gamble intangibles: youth, memories, affection, faces, etc.  Sometimes this can be explicitly temporary (for a time) or permanent.  The thing is gained as a token, which can be taken in by the trickster (gaining the youth, etc), given to another, or held in its token form.
	- you might need the Sticky Fingers ability to take intangibles before you can do this



## Other Magic

### Shadow-calling

- You can call on your Shadow's aid normally.
	- Eager to help you get yourself into trouble, or satisfy an appetite that's harmful or risky in some way.
	- Unlike warriors and shamans, it will even help you escape harm or survive, but the price is always that you ruin or humiliate yourself in a serious fashion (break a friendship, ruin your reputation, violate a trust).
- When you call for aid, you may make it a wager instead of a bargain.  The wager is always a coin flip (or maybe slightly favoring the trickster, as a temptation?  2:1?), and you cannot affect the odds.  If you win the wager, you get he aid without any price.  If you lose, you pay the price, and may pay it again and still get the aid, or choose not to.

### Atavism

- A trickster usually has a primary totem animal, always something clever, quick, sneaky, etc reflective of their personality (mouse, rabbit, crow / raven, monkey, snake, magpie, ferret, spider, fox, mongoose, mockingbird, raccoon, tanuki, etc).
- They may have their atavism as a familiar, or via shapechanging.
- A trickster can spend Fool's Gold or an aspected cypher to borrow other forms (still always tricksy animals) for a while.  This either lets their famiar change shape, or they themselves (even if they can't normally).
- While shapechanging, the trickster can change in and out of animal form with ease and speed.
- As usual, you can borrow the qualities of your atavism while staying in human form.

### Artifice

- you can use your powers with art
- create pocket lie-realms in paintings or music that you can step into
- transmit lies w/ art, graphitti, writing, poems, etc
- compose art of the truth and study the result as a means of divination and insight into the topic
- offer up a piece of art to the muse for Fool's Gold, or use it as stakes in a wager?
- use a work of art as a vector for stealing (a painting takes on the person's face, a poem captures their charisma, etc)
- you can write some of your Shadow Debt into a piece of art and get rid of it, but now there's a bit of embodied malice roaming the world
