## The Poles of the World

### The Fulcrum World

- At the center of the Material world, beneath the eye of the Heavens, above the pit of the Abyss, ringed by the seas of the Horizon, the known world has formed. Called by some the Fulcrum, it is the heart of the Game of Kismet.
- Here, the churns of the elements, frozen in stability and worn by time, has created a number of continents, archipelagos, and chartable seas. The war of the Elder Races further scarred and shaped it.
    - "Elemental hotspots", once great bastions of the djinn, are incorporated into the land, creating regions of strangeness and fecundity (volcanos, seething trackless jungles, glaciers, etc).
    - Shards of a crystalline city and vast works of geological architecture mark the former redoubts and territories of malakim.
    - Winding caverns, cracked rifts, and wastelands surround the ghuls' irruptions of bone, salt, voidstone, and black iron.

### The Horizon

- Chaos lies beyond the sea, beyond the horizon.
- As you sail out into the outer seas, the isles grow stranger and stranger, moving toward the primordial source of the Elements in the far beyond.
- They skies and stars become unfamiliar, strange, and shifting; weather is weirder.
    - The tides, Law's imposition from the Moon upon Chaos's sea, and their currents are a more constant navigational aid than the stars.
- Great primordial leviathans swim.
- Each isle is its own strange amalgam of the elements, less bound to the familiar forms of the world. Time and space can be weird; navigation uses instinct and Chaos, not the stars or predictable currents.
- Isles can often drift (or perhaps the waters do), and may even drift near or into the Fulcrum World, perhaps taking root, crashing into and merging with the existing lands, or floating back away.

### The Heavens

- Law lies beyond the sky, in the clockwork celestial perfection of the spheres and their stars. Genius loci can take hold on isles, ideas that begin to shape a domain.
    - These "isle-laws" could be the basis for conflicts; people come to the isle and conflict with its law, or isle have beef with each other over incompatible law or "legal frameworks/theories", expressed through natural forces and/or the societies on them.
    - This fragmentation is the product of Chaos's influence (via moon, etc) and that this is the Lower Heavens, not the Upper.
- In the Lower Skies, skyships sail between crystal isles. These ships can't remain buoyant in the gravity near the Earth, but can dock at the loftiest mountains (or towers).
    - Air grows thinner the higher you go; the Upper Heavens where the true malakim dwell is nigh-vacuum.
    - In the Lower Skies (again, due to Chaos's influence), the isles swirl in large gyres, kind of like Hadley Cells. They are distributed horizontally, but also stacked in layers, rising toward the Upper Heavens. Isles usually stay in a single gyre. The boundaries between are tough to brave, with storms, turbulent winds, etc. You must find the right currents and times to make the crossing, so a gyre is a natural boundary for societies, ecologies, etc.

- The sky is sometimes the battleground or herald of major plays.
    - Upheavals of the celestial order often signify big plays by Entropy, such as eclipses. Day length varies over the year as Entropy's power waxes and wanes, leeching heat, light, and life from the world (and day length can also suddenly vary to mark major power events; a day without sun, a sun that never sets, etc).
    - The Moon is a battleground between Law (maker of the lesser celestial light) and Chaos (who imposed its changing phases). Law did manage to impose regularity on the phase.
    - Sunspots are incursions from Chaos and Entropy
    - Moon and Sun rise and set because of Entropy's war against them.

### The Abyss / Underworld

- Entropy lies at the base of the Abyss, beneath the world.
- The Underworld is a place where the world crumbles and is drawn down toward oblivion, losing individuality and cohesion. The elements melt and combine in strange and "misshapen" ways outside the paradigm of the overworld. Weird places, monsters, etc. Time and space get weirder and less certain as you go down; things can linger without proper time to end them. There is a stew of decaying matter, dissolving idea, a flotsam of partial form and identity, husks of what was. Echoes of dead cities, stark dying forests, ashen plains, graves and mausoleums without end, like barges carrying their cargo toward the void.
    - The Underworld seeks to suck down the surface world. So long as a city/town/castle is inhabited, kept "alive", it resists the pull. When abandoned or ruined, it begins to sink, sometimes similar to real subsidence/burial, sometime unnaturally fast.
    - Exiled and ousted gods, monsters, races, and other beings may find refuge, if they can eek out survival, though they may become changed over time, eroded, merged with other crumbling things.
    - Earthquakes can occur where Entropy digests a big chunk of reality and the whole world slips "down" a bit, cracking and crumpling. Rifts open to swallow things, buildings are cast down.
    - Vulcanism might be energetic matter trying to burst back out of the underworld? Maybe some kind of Chaos event? Or a slurry of demon energies erupting out to consume the world?

  

- deep in the Underworld, close to the Abyss itself is the "demon swamp" or "cathedral of flesh" where demons spontaneously form from the rotting sludge of reality and hunt and claw their way back upward.
    - perhaps a play of Chaos, a spark of (re)generation even in the depths of entropy?
