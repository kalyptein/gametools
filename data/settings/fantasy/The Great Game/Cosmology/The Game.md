## Cruxes

- plural cruxes / cruces (kruks-es or kru-sees)
- a confluence of the currents of the Great Game, richer with power, likely having lasting / far-reaching (though perhaps subtle) effects. there are cruxes of all different magnitudes
- heroes and gatherings tend to be drawn into cruxes, with or without their intent, willingness, or knowledge; the game sweeps them up.
- cruxes are also where heroes and gatherings are often created, as survivors or unwitting players gain their first spark of power.
- normal rules can bend or break in cruxes. the power granted to a member of a gathering is normally tied to the gathering, and can't be stolen away or changed. In a crux, a traitor might abscond with power, a schism could split a gathering in half, gatherings might merge or have their character shift dramatically, or have power stolen by a rival (to enter a crux is often through of as wagering some of your power; you risk change or diminishment for the prospect of gain). this can also happen to heroes, gods, objects of power, invested locations, etc.

## The Loom of Souls

- Kismet brought forth the Loom of Souls, and each of the Great Gods used it to create one of the Elder Races.
- Afterward, their work allowed Kismet to begin weaving the later mortal races

- The Loom is believed to be an actual artifact, or the physical manifestation of the divine principle. It seems to have cropped up across history, sought by those who craved power to create or reshape life, or to "perfect" their own souls in some fashion.
    - Demon lords seek it to unscramble their essence and reach godlike power, or to stitch stolen essence into themselves. Demonologists have developed the Stitched Path as an echo of the Loom, expressed through demonic consumptive power, grafting stolen soul-essence into themselves.

Hekāyat (حکایت)

- The legend / path of a hero or gathering

Pronunciation:

- Persian: he-kā-YAT (roughly heh-kah-YAHT)
- Arabic: ḥikāya (hi-KAA-ya, with the deeper Arabic ḥ)

Meaning:

- story
- tale
- narrative
- account
- anecdote
- recounting of events

It comes from the Arabic root ḥ-k-y (ح ك ي), meaning “to tell, narrate, relate.”

The nuance of Hekāyat

Unlike a simple “history,” Hekāyat implies a story that has been preserved and retold. It often has a literary, legendary, or moral quality.

Compare:

- History: “What happened.”
- Biography: “The life of a person.”
- Hekāyat: “The tale of what happened and why it matters.”

That makes it especially appropriate for:

- heroes
- saints
- kings
- legendary warriors
- sorcerers
- ascended beings
- demigods

A hero does not merely have a history; they have a Hekāyat.

  
Hekāyat-e Azim “The Tales of the Great Ones.” A collection of myths about the Azimān.

  
Hekāyat-e Kismet “The Tales of Fate.” Could refer to stories of pivotal moments in the Great Game.

## Oracles

Oracles are an eclectic set of unique individuals with insight into Kismet: her cruxes, the hidden moves of her players, and the ways and shapes of numina.

- Some oracles are unique entities, unlike any other race (some are even constructs, elementals), other are members of familiar types (mortals, monsters, demons, malakim, etc). Of the latter, some are known to have a life before becoming an oracle (gaining their status through a crux, or transfer from the prior oracle), others seem as if they have simply appeared, full-formed (some may be fateborn, like hakaan, though in more numerous forms).
- Each oracle is unique (or part of a small, related set), with their own epithet (e.g. the Red Oracle, the Oracle of Sand, the Drifting Oracle, the Oracle of Crawling Shadows, the Oracle Unmoved, etc). Some epithets reappear after the prior oracle's deadth, and some are transferred like a lineage.

Oracles are powerful beings, often with unique forms of power. Most seem to be ageless, but they not unkillable.

- Being ageless, many who are not novel entities are members of the older races (djinn, malakim, ghul, elves, etc)
- A persistent rumor has it that slaying an oracle calls down a curse of ruin from Kismet upon the slayer (and perhaps anyone foolish/unlucky enough to be standing nearby).

Oracles are generally neutral is matters of politics and the pursuit of the game.

- They may have close kept agendas of their own that the further through their aid and advice, but they are known to aid all peoples without favoritism.
- An oracle's personality, payment requests, and the nature of their advice can be quite unappealing to some seekers, making some more in demand to certain folk..

Foremost, they provide insight into matters of the game.

- Their knowledge is imperfect, but much greater than any others.
    - Often they require physical samples or measurements from the vicinity / context of a crux in order to comprehend it more fully. These can be significant objects, blood samples, astrological readings, captives, etc. The seeker is usually tasked with this, though sometimes they (or the seeker) may turn to others, hiring them or calling in a debt.
    - Their pronouncements often take the form of if-then prophecies which describe actions or events in a crux that may have really non-linear effects on the outcome and the world. They are often cryptic, symbolic, and can be quite misleading (e.g. "if the seven eyes behold the death of the king's favorite, the raging boar will surely soar above the capitol" (maybe seven crucial supporters learn of the death of the king's daughter (or secret concubine, or favorite hunting dog), the lord who's banner (or nickname) is the boar will succeed the throne. or maybe not).
- They can also provide sage advice and knowledge, identify legendary artifacts, craft or bestow magical aid, advise on the geomancy of imbued locations, and council how to cultivate power or path to ascendance (of one's self, Gathering, or god). They may even grant mundane aid like hospitality, sanctuary, or material support.
    - Cultivation of power may include the cleansing of deviations.
- Oracles must always be paid for their service, and usually not in simple wealth. This seems to be either a geas or a universal personality trait.
    - They may accept favors, magic items, knowledge, services, rare resource, and more.

Oracles seem to be outside the normal rise and fall of heroes, gatherings, and cruxes.

- They inform on cruxes, and may have designs on how the game plays out, but they do not lead Gatherings or gain further power from the game. Some hint they may gain power by assisting or guiding the game in certain directions.

Most oracles have a sanctum, though some are nomadic, or are encountered within a specific territory.

- Though not Gatherings, they may have small groups of retainers: recruited, created, imbued, or trained. They often have sizeable collections of objects of power, given or acquired as part of requests.

There are also non-oracle scholars of the Game and its power, sometimes called agonosophists, but they lack the special insights and powers of oracles.

## The Great Gods

- Playing the "game of divinity" that shapes the world. All are personified as 'she', but aren't individuals lesser beings interact with.
    - Law: stasis / persistance, structure, hierarchy, relationship; depicted as an ornate, masked queen
    - Chaos: creation, change, impermanence; depicted like a tattooed, primal shamness
    - Entropy / Void: breakdown to nothingness or ground-state, void, change without replacement; depicted as a shrouded woman in black

Sages debate the ultimate nature of power and reality:

- Are the 3 Great Gods the sources of their respective power, with Kismet as a subordinate synthesis?
- Is Kismet the source of all power, with the 3 Great Gods drawing on the resources she provides to play the game?

- Is the whole personification of the Great Gods just a mortal (and ascended being) foible?
